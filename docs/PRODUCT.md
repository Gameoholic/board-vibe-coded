# Product & Design

> **Scope of this file.** Why this exists, who it's for, what it must feel like, and the current product behaviour + visual language. The human side.
> **Write here:** the owner's goals and pain points, product principles, what each current feature *is* from the user's view, deliberate scope decisions, and the design language + its rationale.
> **Don't write here:** how features are implemented (→ `ARCHITECTURE.md`), code style (→ `CONVENTIONS.md`), or unbuilt ideas (→ `BACKLOG.md`).

## What this is (and why)

A personal productivity board replacing a physical whiteboard the owner ran for six months: daily/weekly checklists, a tiered "registry" of habits (workouts, instrument practice, language study, a NASA project, trip planning), each task worth points. Single user, self-hosted, built incrementally. Used **primarily on PC, but also as a mobile app** — so the shared truth lives on the backend, reachable from any device.

**Everything is in service of one goal: making the owner more productive and more consistent.** A feature that doesn't do that doesn't belong.

### The pain points it fights

- **Procrastination** (mainly YouTube), avoided-task backlog, and losing structure around hobbies.
- **Consistency** in habits and calisthenics — showing up repeatedly, not once.
- **Friction to log.** This is the big one. *The physical whiteboard stopped working because of access, not design* — the owner wasn't in the room with it most of the day. The tracking concept was sound; the friction killed it. **So anything that reduces the friction of logging something beats anything that adds a feature.** This is *why* the app must be everywhere (PC + mobile) and why instant feedback matters so much.

## Owner context (about the person, not the code — this doesn't go stale)

- **Burned by a previous vibe-coded project that came out messy.** That's the whole reason this rebuild started from zero and why "one change at a time, no unprompted scope" (see `CONVENTIONS.md`) is non-negotiable. Quality anxiety here is real — take it literally.
- **Loves configurability, genuinely, as an end in itself — but not everywhere.** When in doubt, expose the knob with good UX rather than hardcoding a choice. That said, the owner has deliberately narrowed some scope (tabs are static once created). Configurability is a value, not an absolute — when the owner narrows scope, respect it; don't re-expand it "for consistency."
- **Streaks and consistency mechanics are the strongest motivator** in the original design — a first version now exists (see below); treat this UI as high-stakes, not an afterthought, as it grows.
- **Wants the satisfaction of the physical object preserved.** Don't let this drift into feeling like a spreadsheet or a bare CRUD form. The checkbox weight, the animation, the whiteboard-style `[%]` notation are all in service of that — the live counter + flying-point animation got real polish *because* instant, satisfying feedback is core to why this beats a whiteboard, not a nice-to-have.
- **Wants the cost shown before acting — and notices when a feature is built with that care.** Prune naming, in red, the streak it would break ("Breaks “First streak”") got explicit praise. When an action can hurt something the owner values (a streak, points, a place in a list), say so where they choose it, not after; and small touches like putting a restored task back exactly where it was are what make a feature feel right here.
- **Some items may need to be private eventually** — never rendering their label anywhere on screen. Not built yet; flagged so it isn't forgotten when tasks contain anything the owner doesn't want visible.
- **The board conceptually starts empty.** The current seed data (Reset board, Workout A, Immersion, etc.) is placeholder/demo content standing in for the owner's real tasks — not sacred, not a permanent fixture.

## Current product shape

What exists today, from the user's point of view. (Implementation details for all of this live in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.)

### Navigation & places

The app is no longer only the board — a slim **icon rail on the left edge** switches between "places." Today: **Board** (home, the whiteboard) and **Settings**, plus a **Shop** button. The Shop isn't a separate page — it happens *on the board*: your tabs vanish, the points counter glides to the top-centre, and your shop tabs appear beneath it (going back plays it in reverse). Shop tabs are your own shelves for organising rewards (no difference between them — just grouping), each listing rewards you define with a cost in **points (%)**; the price tag is the buy button. They're the same kind of tab as the board's: drag them anywhere on the shop's canvas, resize them, sort and toggle what shows, drag rewards to reorder them, and bundle rewards into groups — with the same grid, snap and reset controls in the corner (the shop keeps its own arrangement). New shop tabs come from the `+` in that corner; rename, recolour or delete one from its title. **Buying really spends your points** — the counter drops with a visible sting, on the board and in the shop alike, on every device — and a negative total closes the shop. The currency is simply your board points, not a separate coin balance (that split may come later); every purchase is recorded, so the points you've ever *earned* stay knowable apart from what you've spent. What a shop sells, and the weekend sale, are below ("The shop"). The rail is always visible and shows a small dot on Settings when something's waiting there (a day/week to close); on a phone it lies along the foot of the screen instead, under your thumb (see "On a phone"). At its foot, a sun/moon button flips the app between light and dark, and the chevron under it opens every theme: Match system, Light, Dark, Slate, Black, Charcoal, and well-known editor themes (Nord, One Dark, Tokyo Night, Solarized, Gruvbox, Monokai). The theme is per device and follows the system until you pick one; picking a dark theme makes it the one the button flips to. Each canvas also remembers where you left its camera (zoom and position) on this device.

### The shop

- **Three kinds of reward, picked when you add one** (the add form's first row; a reward keeps its kind):
  - **Repeatable** — a video, a show, a film: buy it as often as you like; "Times bought" shows ×N.
  - **One-time** — headphones, a keyboard, a trip: buying asks first ("Buy New headphones for 30%?"), then it shows **Bought** for a beat and **leaves its shelf**, like a one-time task. Its tab's Display has **Already bought** (off by default) to see them hatched at the bottom, each with the day it was bought. It's never bought twice.
  - **Item** — it gives you something on the board: a **Bounty reroll** (banked until used, spent after the week's free ones) or a **Booster reroll**. Picking one names the reward after it (rename it, re-emoji it, price it as you like), and its row shows what you have as two chips: **"2 owned"** (bought) and a green **"1 free"** (the week's). Bought ones carry over from week to week; the week's free Bounty reroll is counted apart and gone when the week ends.
- **Inventory.** The bag in the shop's corner lists every item and how many you have of each.
- **Booster reroll.** A Booster's right-click menu offers **Reroll Booster** (saying how many are left) while you have one and the Registry has another habit to deal: that Booster stops being one, the hand is dealt again over the board without it (and without the week's other Boosters), and you pick one card — the same deal, flip and stamp as the week's.
- **The weekend sale** — the old whiteboard's rule: leisure was **half price at the weekend**, and the weekend starts **Thursday evening**, when the working week is done. Rewards on the sale cost **50% less from Thursday 17:00 until the week is ended**. Like every time on the board it follows your days, not the clock: a day you haven't ended is still today, so Thursday's sale waits for Wednesday to end, and a Saturday night you haven't ended is still on sale. Each reward has a **Weekend sale** switch in its form — on by default for a repeatable reward, off for one-time and item ones (rewards from before kinds were all leisure, so they're on it). It's a modifier on the price, shown the way a task shows one: a green **Sale** tag, a "50% off" line, and the price tag in the sale's green with the old price struck through; beside the points counter, "Weekend sale · 50% off until the week ends". **You pay what you saw**: if the sale starts or ends between seeing a price and buying, nothing is bought and the shop shows the new price. Settings → **Weekend sale**: on/off, the discount, when it starts (a weekday and a time), and **Start the sale now** — for a holiday, say: it asks first ("Start the weekend sale now? It runs until the week ends."), then runs until the week ends.

### Day & week close

The board has a real **end-of-day / end-of-week moment**, like sweeping the physical whiteboard. When you open the app and a new day has begun, a gentle banner asks one plain question in one word — **"Has Friday, October 2 ended?"** — **Yes, end it** locks the day in, **wipes the board for the new one** (a new day unchecks Daily and Registry, a new week unchecks Weekly; Tasks and its Freezer are never wiped) and shows a **recap**, dated the same way, like a mobile game's daily log-in. **A week is never asked about: it ends with the day that ends it** — ending its last day (Saturday, with Sunday weeks), or any later day after time away, ends the week too, and the week's recap follows the day's. **Days you never opened are simply missing**: nothing is recorded for them, the week's recap shows them as "—", and streaks count them as missed — no making up for them. Back after a gap, the day you left open is the one that ends, then its week, and the week you're back in runs from that day to its end (back on a Friday: Friday to Saturday); a week you were away for entirely never opens at all — but streaks still see it, as a week nothing was done in. The prompt then lists **what was skipped since** — "October 4 – October 18 · 15 days", "Weeks of October 4 – October 17 · 2 weeks" — so a long gap is never a surprise. **A day not yet ended is still today**, whatever the clock says: finish Friday's daily at 2am on Saturday and it's Friday's — the streak counts it for Friday at once, Friday's recap has it, and its scheduled boxes are all open (every hour of Friday has come); say "yes, it ended" when you're up. A **day's** is one page: each tab you cleared something in (named, in the tab's colour) with **how many** and **the % it earned** — "Tasks 1 (1.88%)" — then what the day **earned** and **lost** to the shop, and **each purchase**. A **week's** takes over the whole screen as **sticky notes on the board**: the board fades back behind a frosted wash (it keeps showing the old week until the recap closes, so nothing — least of all the new Bounty — is given away early) and the week is stuck onto it, and **Done peels the notes off and drops them** before the wash clears onto the new week. It's pages you flip (Back / Next, or the dots — nothing scrolls): its **days slap on one after another** as sticky notes, each in the colour of the tab it cleared most in, with its count per tab in the tab's colour and its %, what the day earned and lost, and its purchases, then a yellow note for the whole week; then its **streaks**, on one note, each from where it stood as the week began to where it ends it ("5 → 12 +7"; nothing beside one that didn't move); then, if the Freezer did anything, its **frost** — each task on ice, what its frost grew from and to, its bar filling, and a **Subzero** stamp slamming onto one that just filled up — and what the close **froze**, each on its own note with how long it had waited; and last, the **Bounty roll** — a slot reel per Bounty (one after another) that **you swing yourself**: swipe it, with the mouse or a finger, and it's off past a ticking pointer at the pace you gave it, slows down by itself, runs just past the name it lands on and springs back onto it, then bursts with rays and sparks and stamps its ×2 on, saying in plain words what that means ("Finish it this week and earn 2x more points."). Next waits for the reel to land. With nothing on ice there's no Bounty, and its note says so. "Earned" is what the period's ticks gained (an untick of something won earlier counts against its day; the close's own reset never does). No list of every task; the points those tasks held are **banked**, so wiping never costs you anything; **Not yet** defers until you reopen (for late nights before your day-start time). This is what makes streaks honest: a period only counts what was genuinely done when it closed, so a box you ticked then unticked doesn't inflate a streak. When exactly a day and week begin is configurable (see Settings). A **fresh or reset board has no day/week yet**, so it prompts you to *start* your first one (worded "start", not "end") rather than starting silently.

### Settings

A dedicated Settings place (not on the whiteboard, since it's board-wide truth shared across your devices, not a per-screen preference like the canvas layout):

- **Days & weeks** — the timezone, **when a new day starts** (default 00:01; set it to 4am if your day really ends then), and **when a new week starts**: weekday (default **Sunday**, the Israeli week) and time (default 00:01).
- **Wind-down (optional, off by default)** — a screen-off nudge. Set a time you want to be off your screens by and **when the full message pops up** — an editable list (defaults: **30, 15, 5 min before**, and **when I open the board**; delete any or add your own). "When I open the board" shows it on any page load once you're already inside the window. Each pop **grows** with how close the target is — bigger the later it is, taking over the whole screen and dimming the board toward black at the deadline, its top line ticking the **live current time** in red — to physically push you to shut down. Two ways out, stacked in the centre: **Acknowledge** (the big one) winds it down (a satisfying collapse) into a **small burning candle** that floats in a free spot on the board — its wax burns lower as the time nears, still showing the current time — out of your way so you can keep going but always in the corner of your eye, **glowing red once the time is past** (the flame gutters to smoke); click it to bring the full alert back. Below it, a smaller red **Dismiss for today** (with a confirm step) clears it until tomorrow. The headline is customizable (blank = a generic default). It's a pure nudge tied to no task — it never touches points or streaks.
- **Weekend sale** — whether it runs (default on), the discount (default 50%), when it starts (default Thursday 17:00; it runs until the week is ended), and **Start the sale now**. See "The shop".
- **Backups** — everything (tabs, tasks, streaks, shop, settings and all history) is copied beside the board every so often (default every **2 days**, keeping the **5** newest; the oldest goes to make room), the way back from a reset gone wrong. Settings has whether it backs up on its own, how often (6 hours to a week) and how many to keep, then the **saved backups**, newest first, each with when it was taken and how long ago; **hovering one shows that board as it was saved** — its tabs where this device keeps them, their tasks ticked or not, its streaks, and what it held to spend (tap one, on a phone). A backup kept past a lowered limit says it "goes at the next backup". Restoring one isn't built.
- **Reset board** — wipes all tasks, data and history completely, leaving an **empty board** (no tabs/tasks/streaks). Behind a confirm; can't be undone. Destructive and deliberate.

### Sections ("tabs")

- **Static once created** — name and allowed task types can't be edited or deleted from the UI. This was a deliberate scope-narrowing the owner asked for; **don't reintroduce rename/delete/retype UI without being asked again.**
- Two things about a tab stay adjustable: its **color** (click the tab title → inline color popover, applies immediately, no save button) and its **place on the board** — the board is a **free whiteboard-style canvas**, not a fixed row of tiles. Drag the `⠿` handle to move a tab anywhere; drag any corner to resize it (a corner can be grabbed from just outside the tab too) — the corner stays exactly under the pointer and isn't snapped to the grid. A tab fits its tasks until you resize it; from then on it keeps the size you gave it. A corner stops where the tab's tasks just fit; **hold Shift** to go past that, down to just its title, and its list then scrolls inside it (the board stays put; a finger drags the list on a phone). Forms and confirms opened from a row are never cut off by a short tab: they open over everything, at full size even when the board is zoomed out. Positions **snap to a light grid** as you go and spring into place on release, and the last tab you touch floats above the others. You can **pan** by dragging with the **middle mouse button** (or scrolling) — left-click is left free — and **on a touch screen a finger drags the board from anywhere, on top of a tab too**, while a tap still ticks and a hold still opens a row's menu; **zoom out a little** with ⌘/ctrl-scroll, a trackpad pinch or two fingers — but the camera stays clamped to the tabs' area plus a small margin, so there's no getting lost in empty space; dropping a tab further out simply extends the reachable area. This whiteboard layout is **per-device** (a phone keeps its own arrangement, not a wide monitor's).
- **Canvas controls** float in the **top-right corner inside the board** (not a separate bar — just the buttons, pinned there as you pan): one icon button per settings group, each opening its own inline popover — the same pattern as a tab's own color/sort controls. Today that's a **grid** button (snap to grid on/off — off lets tabs land exactly where dropped; grid size S/M/L, governing both snapping and the dot grid; show grid — a faint dot overlay as a placement guide, off by default) and a **reset** button (restores every tab to its default position, confirmed inside the popover). These are preferences, not board data — they live per-device, same as the layout itself, and the toolbar is the natural home for more canvas-wide knobs as they arrive.
- **One tab at a time — the board's other view.** The last button in that corner switches between the canvas and **one tab at a time**: the tab fills the frame (its list scrolls inside it, its `+` stays at its foot), the points counter sits above it as it does in the shop, and a small **deck** in the bottom-right corner stands for your tabs — a pile of plies, one per tab in its colour, the tab you're on on top, each filled as far as that tab is done today (or this week) and filling as you tick. Press the deck for the list of tabs, each name in its colour with what it holds ("2/9" for a tab that starts over, "13" for one that doesn't), and pick one: the deck cuts to it. The shop has the same view and a deck of its own. It's a per-device choice like the arrangement, it remembers the tab you were on, and **a phone starts in it** — a canvas of tabs each as wide as the screen is all panning. One thing is left to the canvas: linking a streak's tasks, since the tasks have to be on screen to drag the line onto.
- **New board tabs can't be added from the app.** The board's tabs (Daily, Weekly, Registry, Tasks, Freezer, Streaks) are created by the server on a fresh board; the shop's tabs are the only ones you add yourself.
- **The tab title itself is the color swatch** (no separate colored dot). The `+` add-task button is deliberately neutral gray, not tab-tinted — it's used rarely and shouldn't compete visually with the colored title or the task rows.

### On a phone

The same app, laid out for a narrow screen and a finger. Nothing here is a separate mobile product, and none of it changes the PC.

- **The places are a bar along the bottom**, and the board starts in the **one-tab view** (see "Sections"), with the points counter at its top.
- **Nothing waits for a pointer.** A row's reorder grip is always there at its left, a tab's `+` is always shown, and a row has no trash: **press and hold a row** for its menu, where Delete is. The menu never fires an action by itself when it opens under your finger.
- **Room for a fingertip.** Rows are a little taller and each box takes a touch from just around it (the boxes look the same as on the PC); the corner buttons are bigger.
- **Forms fit the screen**, typing in one doesn't zoom the page in, and the keyboard pushes an open form up rather than covering its buttons (on Android).
- **On the canvas view** a finger pans from anywhere and two fingers zoom; the corner marks to resize a tab are always visible.
- **Left to the PC:** bundling rows into a new group (dragging the bracket down a row's edge needs a pointer to find it — groups still show, and a row still drags into one), and linking a streak's tasks while in the one-tab view.

### Task types

- **`checkbox`** — done/not-done, optionally carrying a freeform `estimate` string (e.g. `"15–30 min"`) shown beneath the name, an amount (box count), and scheduled times. Used by Daily / Weekly, where a task can legitimately repeat within a period.
- **Prune — "not this time."** A daily or weekly task you've decided to skip can be pruned from its right-click menu ("Prune until tomorrow" / "Prune until next week" — the tab decides which). It leaves the list and **comes back by itself** when that day or week wraps up. Pruning isn't doing: it earns nothing and changes nothing else (ticked boxes stay ticked), and if a streak still needs the task, the menu item says so in red before you click ("Breaks “Morning routine”"). The tab's Display menu has **Pruned tasks** (off by default) to see them dimmed under the list — the rest of the tab still drags and groups — marked "back tomorrow", where right-click → **Unprune** brings one back to its old spot. Only plain daily/weekly checkbox tasks can be pruned — not Registry tiers or tallies, not one-time tasks.
- **`once`** — a one-time task (reschedule the dentist, call grandma for her birthday): **check it and it's gone** — it shows ticked for a beat, then leaves the list. Its points stay yours; it just isn't on the board any more. Same checkbox feel as `checkbox`, but deliberately **without** an amount or scheduled times, in both the add and edit forms — neither means anything for a task that only ever happens once. Used by Tasks. No tab offers a way back to finished ones today (the owner turned the **Completed tasks** toggle off; it can be offered again).
- **Status — what's in play.** A tab can show its tasks in three bands: **In progress** on top (tinted; from its second day a task there says how long it's been in hand — "in hand 3 d" — so a stalled one stands out, while a task started today needs no note, its band says it), the **Backlog** under it, and **Blocked** folded at the bottom as a count ("Blocked · 2", click to open). **The Backlog keeps the order you give it** — drag what matters to the top; new tasks land at its bottom, and a sort ("Get done quick") works inside each band (the priority sorts split it under headers instead — see Priority, next). Move a task with the **status pill** that appears on hover (three icons: in progress, backlog, blocked), with **Status** in its right-click menu (the current one ticked), or by **dragging it onto another band**. **Blocked asks why**: a short note ("the insurance letter"), and/or the task it's waiting on — the row then reads "Waiting on the insurance letter" — linked with the same pick-whip streaks use — and a linked one **unblocks by itself** the moment that task is done, going back to the band it came from. More than 3 in progress gets a gentle "Finish or park one?" — a nudge, never a block. It's the **Status** Display toggle of a tab of one-time tasks (Tasks), on by default — only those tasks have a status. It never touches points, and never moves a task in the tab's order — so a task you park goes back to its old place in the Backlog, and turning Status off shows the plain list exactly as it was. A group whose tasks sit in two bands shows in both.
- **Priority — what matters most.** Every one-time task is **High**, **Medium** or **Low**; a new one is Low. Pick it as you add the task (Low unless you choose another) and change it from the row's right-click menu: **Priority**, the current one ticked, each with its arrows — three red for High, two amber for Medium, one grey for Low. **A High or Medium task wears its colour** on its checkbox and its name, whatever the sort; Low — what every task starts as — stays plain. Two sorts order by it: **Priority: High only** puts the High tasks under a **High** header and the others under **Tasks**, and **Priority: High, Medium, Low** gives each its own. A header says its name in its colour and how many it holds. **The headers split the Backlog** (and the whole list, with Status off); In progress and Blocked are never split — they stay as you dragged them. Under either sort a task still drags among the others of its priority, never into another one. The tab's **Task count** (a Display toggle, on by default) says how many of each under the tab's title — "3 high · 4 medium · 7 low" — or just "7 tasks" when they're all the same priority. Priority never touches points. Only one-time tasks have one — not habits, not pieces — and the Freezer doesn't show it (a task keeps its own for when it thaws).
- **The Freezer — where the tasks you keep putting off go.** Tasks has a second, ice-blue tab, the **Freezer**. A task goes in by hand (right-click → **Freeze**, F) or by itself: **every week close freezes a Backlog task that's waited more than 7 days**. Waiting counts in the Backlog and while Blocked; In progress pauses it (it carries on where it left off — a status change never restarts it), and freezing or thawing starts it again. A Blocked task never freezes (it's waiting on something, not being avoided) and can't be frozen by hand, nor can a Bounty or a piece. **On ice a task gathers frost** — by default 20% of its own points a week, counted by the day and added at each week's end, up to 200% — shown under it as the points it adds ("+0.4% frost") with a bar filling toward the cap, while its bracket shows what it would pay. A task whose frost is full is **Subzero** (a badge on its frost line). Frozen tasks have no status and can't be done: their checkbox is a **thaw** button. **Thawing starts it** — to the top of Tasks, In progress — and it keeps its frost but gathers no more (that week's days so far are lost; freeze it again and it carries on from what it kept). A thaw **pays a small bonus** (0.5% by default) if the task has frost — said in orange under its frost ("+0.5% thaw bonus"), on the thaw button's hover ("Thaw now and get +0.5% for starting it") and in the menu ("Start now for +0.5%"); move it out of In progress before it's done and the bonus is gone for good — only freezing it again earns another. A thawed **Subzero** task pays **at least 100%**, or more if the rest comes to more. Broken down, it's the whole task that's Subzero: its pieces pay its frost as you tick them, and finishing the last one pays whatever brings the whole task up to 100% (the Avalanche plays on the task, not on a piece). A thaw gets an effect sized by how much frost there was, and finishing a frosted task gets one of three — **Crack**, **Shatter**, and the **Avalanche** for Subzero. An **Age** chip beside a task's name says how long it has waited ("today", then days, weeks, months; Display → **Age**), and its hover says when it was created, when it froze or thawed, how long it's been in its status and — turning the chip orange — "Will freeze when week ends" when that's coming. The week's recap shows the frost gathered and what the close froze. **Settings → Freezer**: the days a task may wait (default 7), frost per week (20%), where frost maxes (200%), what a Subzero task pays (100%), and the thaw bonus (0.5%), each frost setting with a line saying how it counts.
- **The weekly Bounty — a reason to face the one you're avoiding.** Every **week close** rolls the week's **Bounties** (one by default) **from the Freezer**, and **you roll them yourself**: the week's recap lays out a reel of what's on ice for you to swipe, and how hard you swing it is how fast, how long and how far it spins before it stops by itself — so your swing decides the Bounty (the reel's order is fixed before it). **A swipe too soft doesn't count**: the reel goes back where it was and asks for a harder one, so it can't be nudged onto a name of your choosing. **Skip** lands it on a random one, at once; it sits just under the prompt and is out of sight until you point at it (on a touch screen, where nothing points, it's always there). A reel you never swung waits: it's shown over the board the next time you open it — and stays up until it's rolled, Skip being the quick way through — and is gone when the week ends. A Bounty is **worth ×2 until the next week close** — miss it and it's gone (it just stops being a Bounty, wherever it is), and a new one is rolled. **It stays frozen until you thaw it** (which starts it, and pays the thaw bonus if it has frost), and once thawed it can't be frozen again. The roll **leans toward what's been on ice longest**: each full week there is one more spot on the reel, and every spot is as wide as any other, so each is as likely to be stopped on. It never holds a finished task, a piece (a broken-down task's Bounty boosts its remaining pieces instead) or one that's already a Bounty — and a reroll's never holds one the week has already had, unless nothing else is left. A crowded Freezer's reel is cut to 48 spots at random. With nothing on ice there's no Bounty that week. A Bounty is **pinned to the top of its list** with a red **Bounty** tag and a "×2 bounty" line under it, and its bracket shows **what it pays, not the sum** — `[2.8%]` in red where it would otherwise say 1.4% (pieces of a Bounty, and the pieces you're still typing under one, show theirs the same way). It's only pinned on screen — it goes back to its own place in the list once the Bounty ends. **Rerolls**, for when it lands on something truly impossible this week — in the recap, or any time from the Bounty's own menu ("Reroll Bounty", saying how many are left): that Bounty is given up there and then, and a new reel without it comes up for you to swing — never once it's won. A week brings its **free rerolls** (spent first, gone when it closes); bought ones (an item in the shop) are kept until used. **Winning one pays ×2 and keeps it**: the Bounty ending later doesn't take the bonus back (unticking does). Winning one gets the bigger celebration — and, if you've asked for it, **a new Bounty rolls in its place**: its reel comes up for you to swing once the win's points have landed. **Settings → Bounty**: whether weeks roll Bounties at all, how many at a time (default 1), the multiplier (default ×2), free rerolls a week (default 1), and whether winning one rolls another (default off).
- **The weekly Booster — a pick of your own.** Every **week close** deals the **Registry's** habits as sticky notes: face up for a moment, then turned over, shuffled and fanned out on the week recap's last page. **You pick one** — hover lifts a card, a click or a tap takes it — and it's turned over to show what the deal put there (the deal is fixed before you pick, so your pick really decides it), risen, flipped with a foil shine and stamped **+0.5%**; then the rest turn over, showing what you could have had. That task is the week's **Booster**: **every tick of it earns +0.5% more** until the week ends (a tally on each tick, a tier once a day). No free rerolls — a bought one re-deals the hand (see "The shop") — and every Registry task has the same chance; a big Registry is dealt nine cards at random. **Skip** passes the dealing by and picks a card at random for you, laid out at once with its stamp — under the prompt, and out of sight until you point at it, like the reel's. The recap's Done waits for your pick, and if the board was left before it, the hand is dealt again over the board, where it stays up until you've picked. A Booster row wears a purple **Boosted** tag, a "+0.5% boost" line, a purple bracket showing what each tier pays, and a purple border around the row — its tag a plain label: nothing glows, moves or flashes. **Settings → Booster**: whether weeks deal one (default on), how many to pick (1–3, default 1), and the bonus (default 0.5%).
- **Break down — a task that turned out bigger.** Right-click a one-time task → **Break down** (B): a line opens under it — type a piece, Enter, the next one, Esc (or Enter on an empty line) when done. Each piece you type shows what it'll be worth before it's created. **Breaking a task down splits its points evenly across the pieces** (on hundredths, and always adding up to exactly what the task was worth: 1% in three is 0.34 / 0.33 / 0.33), so nothing is lost; each piece's points can be changed through its Edit. **A piece added later adds value** — the task really did grow — worth an average one of the pieces already there. Or **tuck an existing task in** by dragging the entry's "Tuck a task in" whip onto it (it keeps its own points). The task then shows its **whole worth** in its bracket, a strip of boxes beside its name (one per piece, filled as they're done) and **what's next** under it ("1 of 4 · Next: Buy brackets"); clicking that line folds the pieces away and back (remembered on this device). **Pieces are real tasks**: each is ticked on its own and **pays as you go**; the last one **finishes the task** with a bigger celebration than a single tick. The task's own box is all of its pieces at once — ticking it ticks every piece left **one after another** (a tenth of a second apart, each paying as it goes), unticking it (in the Completed look-back) reopens them all. However the last piece gets ticked, the finish plays back up: the line the pieces hang from fills toward the task and its own box ticks with a pop and a ring, before the task leaves. A piece can be **Blocked** (the task marks it — "· 1 blocked" — and "Next" skips it), reordered among its siblings by its handle, and taken out again with **Make it its own task** (M), which puts it right after the task it left. One level deep: a piece can't be broken down itself. Deleting a broken-down task takes its pieces with it (the confirm says how many); duplicating one copies its pieces too.
- **`tiered`** — pick the tier you hit (e.g. 1hr/2hr/3hr of practice). Every tier gets the same points builder (time + effort — see "Points") as any other task, both when you add the task and when you edit it — and **editing a task can add tiers ("+ Add tier") and remove them (the ✕ on each, down to the last one)**, exactly as adding one does. If the tier you'd ticked today is the one removed, the tick comes down to the highest tier left. Used by Registry, **uniformly on purpose**: even a single-value item (Max dead hang) is a 1-tier tiered task, not a checkbox — one tier is "just a config with one entry." This was briefly simplified to checkboxes and the owner asked to revert it; **don't re-simplify Registry to checkboxes without being asked.**
- **`count`** — a task you do a set number of times, shown as that many checkboxes (like a mini-registry): "twice this week" is a 2-box count task. **Each box ticked earns the task's points** (so a 2-box, 1% task is worth 2% fully done), and it's only "done" when every box is ticked. The amount is editable through the UI (add form, or right-click → Edit…). Built for weekly tasks that repeat a fixed number of times.
- Tiered and count rows show one checkbox-styled square per tier/box (whiteboard-accurate — the physical board had a row of boxes per level).
- **Timer (per-tab Display toggle).** Turning on **Timer** for a tab shows a small stopwatch under every task in it — start/pause/reset, or type a number of minutes. On a **tiered** task it does more: it can auto-select a tier from elapsed time (only ever *advancing* a tier, never downgrading a manual pick — "never in the way") and offers a **Submit** that locks in the reached tier. On any other task it's just a stopwatch and never touches points. It's off by default and, like the other Display toggles, is a per-screen view choice (resets on reload).
- **Scheduled times ("do this at a certain hour").** Any checkbox task — plain or multi-box — can schedule each box so it only unlocks when its time comes. A locked box shows a **lock** with an "Unlocks …" tooltip and can't be ticked early; it opens on its own when the time arrives (no reload needed). The form adapts to the **tab**: in a **daily** tab each box just takes a time of day (a cream at 10:00 every day); in a **weekly** tab each box takes a weekday and an *optional* time (teeth plates: box 1 Friday, box 2 Saturday; or "unlock on Friday, any time" if you leave the time blank). Daily-vs-weekly is inferred from where the task lives — it's **not** an option you pick per task — and there's deliberately **no separate "scheduled" task type**. The lock is a gentle nudge, never a hard rule: it never affects points.

### Points

- **A task's points come from its time and its effort** — the points builder in every add and edit form, read as one sentence: **`1h at Ugh difficulty = 3.75%`** (hours × 2.5% × the effort's multiplier). Click the time and just type one (`45m`, `1h30`, `1:30`, `90` — the field is ready as it opens) or pick a preset; click the effort to pick a level, each shown with its multiplier (×1.5). Or type a `%` of your own over it: the `=` turns `≠`, the picks go dashed, and **Or use 3.75%** under the line puts the builder's back — and the line never moves while you type. A tiered task gets one sentence per tier, under its "Tier 1" head. The forms go in the order they're filled: the name, then its points (or tiers) right under it, then what only some tasks have (boxes, scheduled times, a one-time task's priority), and last the **description**, folded to a "+ Description" line until you want one (and open already when the task has one). Effort is how much you have to push yourself to do it, not how fun or complex it is: **Casual ×0.75** (takes nothing out of you, fun or not — reminding someone of something, planning a trip), **Normal ×1**, **Ugh ×1.5** (you don't want to do it — working out), **Dread ×2.5** (you really hate it, or it's outside your comfort zone). Normal is picked by default. **Settings → Points** renames and reprices the levels and the % per hour, repricing every task still on the builder (shown first, old → new). Effort is part of the task's own points, not a modifier, so it adds no tag or colour.
- **Every task has a points value (`%`)**, shown whiteboard-style in brackets before the name: `[0.2%] Reset board`. Tiered tasks show one per tier: `[1%/2%/3%] Immersion`. This text is plain near-black, never tab-colored — the tab color shows on the checkbox and the tab title instead (a High or Medium task's checkbox takes its priority's colour — see Priority) — unless a modifier changes what the task pays (below).
- **Modifiers — what changes a task's pay, game-style.** The Bounty, frost, Subzero and the Booster are the first of these (and a reward's price takes them too — the weekend sale, see "The shop"), and they all show the same way: a tag after the name (**Bounty**, **Frosted**, **Subzero**, **Boosted**), a line under it in the modifier's colour and icon ("×2 bounty", "+0.8% frost", "=100% subzero"), and a bracket showing what the task pays — in the modifier's colour, or a blend of their colours when there are several (a frosted Bounty's is red to blue). They stack: a Bounty doubles a task's points *and* its frost, and Subzero lifts a task to 100% without lowering one already worth more. Bounty and Subzero rows also glow with a look of their own (a Bounty's embers, Subzero's drifting snow). A finished task keeps what it was paid at, even after the Bounty ends or a setting changes.
- Numbers are trimmed, not fixed-decimal: `0%`, `1%`, `1.5%`, `1.52%`, never `1.50%` — and never more than two decimals: a value kept as 1.646% shows as `1.65%` (points are still kept and added up in thousandths; only the display rounds).

### The live points counter (the signature feel)

- A red HUD widget that sums every task's current value — a completed checkbox's points, a tiered task's selected-tier points, a count task's ticked boxes × its points. Positioned to sit near where the owner's eyes actually are (it moved from a fixed top-right corner per owner request), and it stays visible while feeling like an object in the scene rather than a sticker on the glass.
- Completing a task **flies the row's `[%]` bracket into the counter** with an arc, a scale-pulse, and a particle burst — and the celebration scales with how much you just earned (a tiny 0.08% tick and a 3-hour block should not feel identical). This whole interaction is meant to feel game-like and satisfying — **keep that spirit if you extend it.**

### Forms & interactions

- **All forms are inline anchored popovers, Notion-style — never a centered modal with a dark backdrop.** Any new "create/edit X" UI uses the shared `Popover`, anchored to the button that opened it.
- **Right-click any row — a task, a streak or a reward — to open its actions menu** (on a phone, press and hold). **Edit** is always first and already highlighted, so right-click → Enter edits; each kind then adds what it can do, and **Delete** is always last. Tasks add **Duplicate**: an unticked copy lands right below the original, in the same group — and, where the tab shows Status, the three **Status** choices (see "Task types"), the three **Priority** choices on a one-time task, and **Freeze** where the tab has a Freezer (greyed, saying why, for a task that can't go). On ice the menu is Edit, **Thaw** (saying what it gains), Reroll Bounty for a Bounty, and Delete. One-time tasks add **Break down**, and a piece of one offers **Make it its own task** and **Blocked** instead of the three Status choices (see "Task types"). The Status choices are one set, captioned **STATUS** in the menu (in the bands' small caps), so they read apart from Break down beside them without another separator line; the Priority choices are a set of their own right under them, captioned **PRIORITY**. Every action has a key while the menu is open (E, B, M, F, T, R, D, Del). Right-clicking a tally box still just takes one off, and right-click inside a form or text field keeps the browser's own menu (so paste works).
- **Every tab's Sort and Display menus work the same way, and offer what makes sense for that tab.** What it's likely to use is pinned up front; what's only sometimes useful waits under **Show more**; what means nothing there isn't offered at all (no "Date added" or Age on Daily, Weekly or the Registry; no "Incomplete first" on Tasks, where done tasks leave). The pin beside each option pins or unpins it **for that tab** (on this device), and an option that's in use always shows up front. Some options take the tab's own words: Tasks sorts by **Age** (how long a task has waited, as its Age chip shows), and in the Freezer the same is **Days frozen**. What each tab offers today:
  - **Daily / Weekly** — Sort: Manual, Get done quick, Effort: lightest first (then Effort: hardest first, Incomplete/Completed first, Points, Time). Display: Grouping (on), Time estimate, Pruned tasks (then Timer, Add task button).
  - **Registry** — Sort: Manual, Incomplete first (then Completed first, Points, Effort). Display: Timer, Grouping (on) (then Time estimate, Add task button).
  - **Tasks** — Sort: Manual, Priority: High only, Priority: High, Medium, Low, Get done quick, Age: oldest, Effort: lightest first (then Effort: hardest first, Age: newest, Points, Time). Display: Status (on), Age (on), Task count (on), Add task button (on) (then Time estimate, Timer, Grouping).
  - **Freezer** — Sort: Manual, Days frozen: most (then Days frozen: fewest, Points, Time, Effort). Display: Days frozen, Frost bonus, Frost bar, Thaw bonus (all on) (then Time estimate, Grouping).
  - **Streaks** — Sort: Manual, Streak: high to low (then low to high). Display: Grouping (on), Add streak button.
  - **Shop tabs** — Sort: Manual, Can afford first (then Cost, Most bought, Date added). Display: Note (on), Times bought, Already bought (with one-time rewards), Grouping (on) (then Add reward button).
- **Each tab's "+" is a Display toggle too — "Add task button"** ("Add streak button", "Add reward button"): on, the + always shows at the foot of the tab; off, it's hidden until you point at that spot. **On by default in a tab of one-time tasks** (Tasks — adding is the main thing you do there), off everywhere else.
- **Grouping is every tab's Display toggle** (tasks, streaks, rewards). On, its groups show, each row has the small **[** handle — press and drag it down across rows to bundle them into a labeled group — and a row can be dragged into a group. Off, the tab's groups **don't show at all** (one flat list; they're kept, and come back when it's on) and none can be made. It's **off by default in Tasks and the Freezer** and on everywhere else.
- **Hints and form messages are the board's own.** Every hover hint is the same small dark bubble, and a form that can't be saved says why in that bubble, under the field, in the board's words — "Give it a name", "Pick a time, or type a %", "Link at least one task". Never the browser's grey hover box or its "Please fill out this field."
- **Deleting a task asks for confirmation** via an inline popover, not a browser `confirm()` — the same confirm whether you use the row's trash button or the menu's Delete.
- **A completed task looks "disabled"** (dimmed with a gray hatch overlay), not struck-through or red-tinted.

### Streaks

- A dedicated **Streaks tab** that works like the other tabs (color, drag to reorder, bundle streaks into labeled groups the same way tasks and rewards do, add/edit/delete via the same inline popovers). Each streak tracks how many periods in a row you've kept it up, shown as a **flame + count** badge that reads lit (kept up this period), grace (count holds but this period isn't done yet — not a break), or empty. Daily/weekly streaks also show a small **best** badge — the longest run you've ever reached (your record), which the current run has to beat.
- A streak is **daily or weekly**, and is **mapped to one or more tasks** through a **Notion-style condition builder**: each tracked task is a condition row, and the rows are joined by one shared **AND / OR** connector (the first join is a toggle; the rest mirror it) that decides whether **all** of the tasks or **any** of them counts. Tasks are linked with an **After Effects–style pick-whip**: press the handle in the form and drag a line onto the actual task row anywhere on the board — the target row highlights and the line snaps onto its checkbox, and releasing links it. For a **count** task you can aim the whip at a *specific box* to require that many completions this period (drop on box 2 of 3 → "do it twice"), or at the row as a whole to require **all** of its boxes — the "all" option tracks the amount live, so it stays correct even if you change the count later. Re-dropping an already-linked task (shown amber) updates its required amount. **A streak only takes tasks on its beat**: a daily streak a daily tab's tasks, a weekly one a weekly tab's — the whip turns red over any other, saying why ("Daily streaks only take daily tasks"), and won't link it; and no streak takes a one-time task, in Tasks or the Freezer ("Streaks don't take one-time tasks"); switching a streak's type marks the tasks it no longer fits, and it won't save until they're gone. (A weekly task stays ticked all week, so a daily streak would count it every day; a daily one is unticked before its week ends, so a weekly streak would never see it; a one-time one would count for ever.) A counter counts ticks, so it takes a daily or a weekly task. Everything is a knob, per the owner's "everything customizable." **Streaks have no color of their own** — the flame badge takes the Streaks tab's color.
- Every streak has a **"Count from"** toggle: **Now** (default — from when you set it up) or **All time** (reaches back through the linked tasks' whole history, trustworthy because closed days/weeks only count what was actually done at close — see "Day & week close"). For a **counter** this picks between the **live count** of currently-ticked boxes ("Now") and the **lifetime total** of everything ticked across all days ("All time"), where an accidental tick you undid before the day closed doesn't count but a day that genuinely ended with 2 of 3 boxes ticked adds 2.
- **Backfill (Settings).** Each streak can carry values over from **before the app** — e.g. runs you kept on the physical whiteboard. In **Settings → Streak data backfill** each streak has a **Start** (added on top of the current count, but only when it counts from **All time** — a **Now** streak starts fresh and ignores it) and, for daily/weekly, a **Best** (a record floor: the shown best is the higher of it and what you've actually reached in the app — never added, just a starting record to beat).
- Task-based streaks are the only way to match. (An earlier "Filter (debug)" preview mode was removed from the form — it never tracked anything; a future tag-based filter would return through `BACKLOG.md`, not a dead toggle in the UI.)
- Streak counts use the owner's timezone (**Israel**), always.

### What points do (so far)

Your points are what your checked tasks hold, plus what past days/weeks banked when they wiped the board, minus what you've spent in the **Shop** (see above). Within the current day/week they're still live, so unchecking a task takes its points back off, even after you've spent them; once a period closes and banks them, they're yours. The XP/Coins split remains a `BACKLOG.md` item.

## Design language

A **clean, modern-SaaS** style — deliberately *not* an earlier warm-linen "Studio"/whiteboard aesthetic the owner tried and moved away from on purpose.

- Light neutral background, white bordered cards, **Urbanist** font.
- **Themes are colour only, never a different design.** Dark themes are the same clean board with the neutrals turned around; the owner asked for popular dark variations and editor classics, **nothing novelty or pink**. Paper objects (the recap's sticky notes, Booster cards) stay pale paper in any theme.
- The browser-tab icon is the board's ticked bracket `[✓]`, handwritten like marker on the old whiteboard, in ink (light ink in a dark browser). Picked by the owner from a set of options (https://claude.ai/artifact/A1mYFZD8WxE9jSrVYr6EXd, "Messy").
- Tab identity comes through color (the tab title is the swatch; checkboxes and accents pick it up).
- Motion is intentional and consistent — reuse the existing easing choices and the transform-based animation approach rather than introducing a one-off per feature.
- **Celebration moments go all in.** Anything "extra", a reward, a reveal or a win (the Bounty roll, a recap, finishing something big) gets game-feel, like a good mobile game's reward screen. The owner's reference is the **Bounty reel**: a **build-up** (you swing the reel, and it spins past a pointer that ticks at each name, blurring with its speed), a **landing with weight** (it coasts just past the winner and springs back), then a **payoff** (rays, sparks, a little shake, the result stamped on), and **only then the words** saying what it means. Everyday interactions stay quick and quiet; the big treatment is for the moments that earn it. **Theme it to the board** (a whiteboard's marker, eraser, sticky notes), never to an unrelated literal metaphor (the week recap's first flood was literally water and was sent back for that), and keep the clean look below.
- **The owner cares specifically about code quality and visual style being taken seriously — not just "does it work."** Match existing component structure, CSS-variable usage, and motion choices. A one-off approach for a single feature is a smell here.

If a description-based visual change gets rejected once, **build a live comparison the owner can see rather than guessing again** — this is a learned lesson (see the counter's history in `ARCHITECTURE.md`).
